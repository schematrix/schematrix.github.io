Changelog

Release History

Major versions of this human, in chronological order.

v0.1 · 1990s

Initial commit

First boot in Bursa. 100% organic hardware, zero documentation. Started the way all the best universes do: tiny, loud, and expanding fast.

v0.4 · middle school

Warranty: voided

Disassembled every remote control in the house to see what was inside. Reassembly success rate: ~60%. The leftover screws have never been explained.

v0.5 · adolescence

Unstable release

Hormone drivers installed themselves overnight. The voice module switched frequencies without notice, the hair subsystem went rogue, and every log from this period is best filed under "undefined behavior".

v0.6 · high school

Physics: unlocked

Discovered that math and physics are cheat codes for the real world. Picked the electron path — a few fuses were harmed in the making of this decision.

v0.7 · first loves

Pairing attempts

Several romantic connections established; all eventually timed out. Learned a lot about handshake protocols, unexpected disconnects, and why you should never promise 100% uptime.

v0.9 · the philosophy era

Existential kernel panic

Read nonstop — cosmology, biology, philosophy — slid into a full ontological crisis and wrote a book to debug it. Concluded that one very loud instant plus a few billion years of iterative refactoring explain this entire changelog just fine, no external project manager required. Several builds from this era were compiled under heavy alcohol influence; all have since been deprecated.

v1.0 · university

Soldering iron: acquired

Started Electrical & Electronics Engineering at university. First solder burn, first all-nighter, first "it works but I don't know why".

v1.2 · side quest

Teaching tiny hackers

Taught robotics & coding to kids for 6 months. They asked the best questions and broke things faster than any test rig.

v1.4 · internships

First taste of industry

Internships at a power utility and an automotive R&D lab — then graduated as an EEE engineer.

v1.5 · military

Mandatory system update

Military service: the one update you can't postpone forever. Learned discipline, very short haircuts, and that "hurry up and wait" is a universal protocol.

v2.0 · day job

Professional lamp breaker

Joined a Tier-1 automotive supplier as Embedded SW Test & Validation Engineer. HiL rigs, CAPL scripts and 9 OEM programs at once — brands you'd recognize from the highway.

v2.x · now

Schematrix: the night shift

Nights and weekends became a studio: Meschy, an AI camera, this site — and a growing pile of prototypes.
